Project 1

Vendor Risk Assessment

Action Outfitters, a fast-growing outdoor gear retailer, needs a payment processor before its busiest season and ahead of a move into Europe. I took the vendor from intake to recommendation: business context, inherent risk, a security questionnaire checked against attestations, a scored risk register and conditions for approval.

Business context
Volume500,000 to 700,000 transactions a year
Payment typeOnline, card not present, card and mobile payments. Domestic today.
PrioritiesPCI DSS, GDPR, uptime, data privacy, easy integration
ConstraintsNo card data stored by the retailer. Little tolerance for downtime next quarter. EU expansion next year.
  1. Customer places an online order
  2. Customer is redirected to a vendor-hosted payment site
  3. Payment site sends the information to the payment processor
  4. Processor verifies with the card issuer or bank and obtains authorization
  5. Payment is approved and settled into the merchant account
Vendor intake and inherent risk

Criticality: Critical. Payment processing drives order fulfillment and revenue, and the vendor handles customer PII, card data and order information.

The vendor is recommended as the payment processor.

After assessment of vendor security risk, Payment Vendor Enterprise Payments is recommended as Action Outfitters’ payment processing vendor.

Payment Vendor Enterprise Payments is a platform from Payment Vendor Inc that supports seamless, hosted payment gateway and payment processing on merchant ecommerce sites. Through due diligence review of compliance documentation and attestations (SOC2 Type 2, PCI-DSS AOC, Payment Vendor Legal Center), we deem Payment Vendor to be a mature payment processor with strong information security capabilities.

By implementing a redirect from Action Outfitters’ payment site to a vendor-hosted payment page all customer PII and card information is handled by Payment Vendor end-to-end, minimizing Action Outfitters’ risks of holding sensitive data. Once customer data is entered into the payment gateway, card information is tokenized and sent to payment processing via secure web connection with encryption.

This initial assessment determined the security risk of Payment Vendor Enterprise payments to be moderate. Seven residual risks were identified, with four rated low, and three rated moderate, based on a semi-quantitative scale from 0-100.

Conditions of approval

Action Outfitters should proceed with the following conditions:

  • Get documented incident notification procedures from the vendor. Ask the SLA to set a 24 to 48 hour notification window for security incidents in the cardholder data environment, and name Action Outfitters’ notification contacts.
  • Get an attestation that disaster recovery hot site security controls match or exceed the primary site.
  • Verify the vendor’s encryption standards for data at rest.
  • Request a formal cardholder data retention policy and verify it every year.
  • Investigate and confirm that the vendor’s incident response policy and procedures are reviewed and tested at least annually.
  • Continue investigating and assessing the vendor’s subcontractors.
  • Monitor uptime and outages of the vendor’s subcontractors.
  • Reassess every year using the vendor’s SOC 2 Type 2 report and PCI DSS Attestation of Compliance (PCI DSS 12.8.4).
  • Keep a written agreement that sets responsibility for cardholder data security and accepts liability (PCI DSS 12.8.2).
  • Pursue Action Outfitters’ own PCI DSS Level 3 compliance and complete SAQ A. Assumption: payment processing is handled by a redirect to a vendor-hosted payment page.
  • Request documented access control reviews for subcontractor users, ideally matching or exceeding the frequency of internal access reviews.

EU actions before expansion

  • Keep a data processing register that assigns responsibility for customer card data processing between Action Outfitters and the vendor (GDPR Article 30).
  • Appoint an EU and UK representative for Action Outfitters as a data controller in Europe (GDPR Article 27).
  • Tell customers that the vendor is an independent controller processing their data and explain their data subject rights.
  • Run a Data Protection Impact Assessment and map data flows that involve EU customer card data.
